# Even Money

**A software company that builds poker games and the products around them.**

---

## The name

**Even Money.**

In poker it is the plainest bet there is: 1:1, no edge to either side. It is also what a
player says when they mean *this is fair* — an even-money proposition. One term, doing
both jobs: the house takes no hidden edge, and neither does anyone sitting at the table.

It is poker-native without being poker-coded. Nobody needs to know the game to hear
"even money" and understand that nothing is being taken off the top. It parents a family
of brands cleanly — *an Even Money game*, *an Even Money product* — which matters when
there are ten of them and only one reputation.

It also says what we refuse. A company whose name is a fair bet cannot ship a rigged one.

## What we are

We build poker software: the games people play, and the tools they get better with. Ten
brands, one company, one set of promises. [pip](https://github.com/playpip/pip-web) —
single-player Texas Hold'em, play money, no account, open source — is the one in front of
you.

The brands differ. What they share is not a look. It is that every one of them is the same
kind of honest.

## Why we are different

Four claims, in the order they matter.

### 1. The highest trust in the category

**Nobody at our tables is playing a different game.** No cheating, no collusion, no tool
reading the board for someone while their opponent reads it themselves. Not "we discourage
it" — we do not allow it, and we say how we know.

This is the whole business. Poker software is a category where the player's default
assumption is that they are being had, and that assumption is usually correct. The free
apps look like a scam because they are designed to feel like one. Trust is not our nicest
attribute; it is the only reason a person hands us their attention.

It extends past cheating. No manufactured urgency. No near-miss theatre. No countdown that
is not a real clock. A product that engineers the feeling of almost-winning is cheating the
player just as surely as a bot is, and it is doing it from our side of the table.

### 2. Coaches work here, and players learn here

Most poker products treat learning as a funnel into playing. We treat it as the point.
Coaches run their practice on our software. Players study on it. The same hand a coach uses
to teach is the hand the student misplayed last night, in the same interface, with the same
numbers.

That is why cheating is the line and coaching is not. Someone helping you understand a spot
after the fact makes you a better player. Someone feeding you the answer during the hand
makes everyone else's game a lie. The distinction is not subtle and we hold it precisely.

### 3. Every level, in the same place

A person learning which hand beats which, and a person hunting a leak in their turn play,
are using the same product. Not a beginner version and a pro version — the same tables,
with the scaffolding a beginner needs available and out of an expert's way.

Poker's own saying carries this: *a chip and a chair* is all anyone needs. We build as
though that is true, because it is the reason the game is worth playing at all.

### 4. One AI and ML core, pooled across every brand

The opponents, the coaching, the hand analysis and the spot generation come from one
shared core rather than ten separate efforts. Ten brands' worth of play, feeding one set
of models, serving all ten back.

That pooling is the compounding advantage: the tenth product launches with the accumulated
understanding of the first nine. And because the core is ours, we can say exactly what the
AI opponents can and cannot see — which is the only way claim 1 survives contact with a
machine that plays well.

## Voice

Plain and exact. We state what happened and what it costs.

We do not congratulate a player on losing. We do not say "unlucky!" or "so close". We say
the pot went the other way and what it was worth. The honesty *is* the product, so the copy
cannot be the place where we start performing.

Numbers are typeset to be read under time pressure: monospace, aligned, never jittering
while someone decides. A figure that moves while you are reading it is a small dishonesty.

## What the brand refuses

Inherited wholesale from the design system, because a refusal list that lives only in
design gets overruled by someone with a number to move.

- Green felt, wood grain, gold anything. The casino look is a costume worn by products
  that need one.
- Coin showers and celebrations that outlast the information.
- Countdown urgency that is not a real clock.
- Near-miss framing of any kind, on any surface, including marketing.
- Any mechanic whose honest description would embarrass us.

Each of these is easy, and each converts a game into a machine for extracting one more
hand.

## How it looks

The house design language is **Night Shift**, and it is an argument rather than a palette.

Poker is played one-handed, in poor light, late. So the room is a warm dark you can sit in
for an hour, never pure black, and exactly one cool accent — `--ns-signal` — means *the
clock is waiting on you*. Win, loss and warning carry their own colours precisely so the
signal never shares duty. The moment it means three things it means nothing, and the player
loses the one cue that tells them to act.

Cards are the single bright surface in the room. Chips are flat discs with their value
printed, because colour alone is not a label. Motion reports state and nothing else: a
screenshot of the table looks identical to the table at rest.

Sub-brands may differ in character. None of them gets to break the signal law, the
accessibility floor, or the refusal list.
