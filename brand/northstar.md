# Even Money — North Star

> ## Nobody at our tables is playing a different game.

One sentence, and every product decision answers to it.

---

## What it means

Two people sit down. Whatever separates them — a decade of study, a coach, a bankroll, a
first hand ever played — it is **skill and luck, and nothing else**. No one has a tool the
other cannot see. No one has information the game did not give them. No one is being
steered by a mechanic designed to move a number.

It covers the obvious case: a bot, a collusion ring, a solver open in the next window. It
also covers the case the industry pretends is different — a product that engineers the
sensation of nearly winning is playing a different game from the player, and it is the one
holding the cards.

---

## Why this sentence and not "be trustworthy"

Because it can be violated, and you can tell when it has been.

"Trust" is a feeling and cannot be shipped against. *Nobody is playing a different game* is
a claim about the world with an observable truth value. It settles arguments: when someone
proposes a feature, the question is not whether it is popular but whether it hands one
party a game the other is not in.

It also tells you what is **allowed**, which is the half that matters for our business.
Coaching is allowed — a coach helping you understand a spot afterwards changes your skill,
and skill is what the game is made of. A hand-strength hint for a beginner is allowed, if
the table knows who has it and it is on offer to everyone. Reading the board for someone
mid-hand is not. The line is not "help versus no help", it is **inside the hand versus
outside it, and visible to all versus visible to one**.

---

## What it forbids, specifically

- Any tool that acts on a live hand for one player and not the others.
- Any AI opponent that sees what a human opponent could not.
- Any dark pattern: manufactured urgency, near-miss framing, celebration that outlasts the
  information.
- Any mechanic whose honest description would embarrass us in front of the player it is
  aimed at.
- Any asymmetry we cannot explain plainly on a page anyone can read.

---

## How we would know it is true

A north star that cannot be measured is a slogan. These are the readings:

| What we watch | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| Detected assistance inside a live hand | Zero, and a published account of how detection works |
| AI opponent information set | Documented, auditable, provably no wider than a human's |
| Learner retention at every skill level | New players stay without being funnelled |
| Coach-run practices on the platform | Growing — professionals stake their reputation on it |
| Dark patterns shipped | Zero. Counted, not assumed |
| Player-reported "was that honest?" | Approaching nothing, and answered in public when raised |

The first and last are the real ones. The rest are how you keep them.

---

## The ten-brand test

Ten brands is ten chances to break this once and lose it everywhere. The reason the AI and
ML core is pooled is not only efficiency — it is that **one core can be audited and ten
cannot**. A shared model whose information set is documented is a promise we can keep. Ten
teams each making their own opponent is a promise we can only hope about.

Every brand inherits the north star. None of them gets a local exception.

---

## The trade we accept

This costs us things, on purpose.

It costs engagement mechanics that work. It costs the retention lift that urgency and
near-miss framing reliably produce. It costs the convenience of shipping an opponent that
peeks in order to feel sharper.

We give those up because the category's default is to take them, and a player's default
assumption is therefore that we have. Being the one company where that assumption is wrong
is worth more than any of them — and it is only worth something if we never spend it.

---

## The test, restated for the room

**If a player cannot tell whether a hand was honest, nothing else we built matters.**
